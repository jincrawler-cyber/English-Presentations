Stage 3 · Vocabulary practice · 10 min

Mime the job

  1. Make teams of 4 or 5.
  2. One student chooses a job. Don't say it!
  3. Mime the job. No words, no sounds.
  4. Your team asks: "Are you a chef?"
  5. Answer: "Yes, I am!" or "No, I'm not."
  6. Correct answer = 1 point. Change student.
Stage 4 · Grammar presentation · 15 min

A profile: capital letters, periods and but

A profile is a short text about you: your name, where you're from, where you live, your job, your company or school, and your contact information. It can be a work profile or a personal profile.

Example profile

Ask: Is it a work profile or a personal profile? How do you know?

Use capital letters (A, B, C…)
  • Ifor I (I'm), always. I live in Aguascalientes.
  • Anafor names of people
  • UNIDfor names of places, companies and schools
  • Mexicanfor nationalities and languages (Spanish, English)
  • My…at the beginning of sentences
Use a period ( . ) at the end of statements. Emails stay in small letters: ana_lopez@unid.mx
Use but to connect two different ideas
I'm Peruvian,butmy home is in the United States.
I'm from Zacatecas,butI live in Aguascalientes.
I'm a teacher,butI'm a student, too.

Concept check: Is "I'm from Zacatecas" the same idea as "I live in Aguascalientes"? (No → different ideas → but). Note the comma before but.

People say I'm from Paris (city of origin) and I live in Paris (city now). Paris is my home now = I live in Paris.
